### Procedure

#### 1. Ligand protonation state enumeration

Ligands from the SDF file were processed to enumerate protonation states appropriate for pH 7.4 using RDKit and Dimorphite-DL. Individual SDF/mol2 files were generated for each ligand protonation state for use in docking.

**Rationale.** Correct protonation states at physiological pH ensure accurate representation of ligand ionization and improve docking binding affinity predictions.

#### 2. Molecular docking with pose generation

Each protonated ligand was docked against the best identified ligandable pocket using AutoDock-Vina. The top 5 binding poses per ligand were collected and scored.

**Rationale.** Molecular docking predicts how ligands bind to the target protein and generates binding poses ranked by predicted affinity, essential for identifying active compounds.

#### 3. Binding free energy calculation

Binding free energies (ΔG) were calculated for the best docking poses using MM-GBSA endpoint calculations. Ligands were ranked by predicted ΔG values.

**Rationale.** MM-GBSA provides refined binding affinity estimates from docking poses, improving discrimination between active and inactive ligands.

#### 5. Protein preparation and protonation

Missing residues and atoms were identified and added using PDBFixer. The C-terminal disordered tail (residues 281-314) was excluded from loop modeling due to length and disorder. Short loop residues 182-184 were retained. Hydrogens were added at pH 7.4. A clean receptor file containing protein atoms only (ATOM records) was generated by removing HETATM ligands and water molecules.

**Rationale.** Proper protein preparation with accurate protonation at physiological pH is essential for reliable docking predictions. Excluding highly disordered regions prevents artifacts in modeling.

#### 6. Ligand binding pocket identification

Fpocket pocket detection algorithm was applied to the prepared receptor to identify and rank ligandable pockets on the protein surface. Pocket detection parameters included minimum alpha size 3, maximum alpha size 6, and minimum 15 spheres per pocket.

**Rationale.** Identification of the best ligandable pocket focuses docking efforts on the most probable binding site and improves accuracy of pose prediction.
