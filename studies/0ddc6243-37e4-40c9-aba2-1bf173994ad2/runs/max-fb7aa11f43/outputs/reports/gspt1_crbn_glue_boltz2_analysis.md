## Summary of findings

1. **Ligand binding to CRBN is consistent and chemically plausible.** W344, W350, W364 contact the glutarimide in all three models (minimum distances 2.36–2.91 Å). The CRBN drug-binding domain is correctly identified.

2. **The molecular glue bridging geometry is absent.** The ligand is 17–19 Å from GSPT1 in every model. No ternary bridging contacts were predicted.

3. **GSPT1 and CRBN form protein–protein contacts**, but the interface involves CRBN N-terminal residues (1–145), not the canonical neosubstrate recruitment surface. Model 0 has the largest interface (100 Cα pairs, min 3.84 Å).

4. **High model diversity (RMSD 34–42 Å) signals low confidence** in the global ternary arrangement. The three samples sample different relative orientations of the two proteins, consistent with an unconstrained co-folding search that has found no dominant ternary energy minimum.

5. **No per-residue pLDDT is available.** The DesignWriter outputs a uniform placeholder value of 80.0 for all tokens; this cannot be interpreted as a confidence score.

**Interpretation:** The CC-885-type glue occupies the CRBN pocket correctly, but Boltz-2 does not predict the bridging conformation that would assemble the productive GSPT1-recruiting ternary complex. This could reflect genuine inability of the ligand to simultaneously engage both surfaces at the input SMILES level, absence of the key GSPT1 degron (only the G-loop fragment is present in the 199 aa construct), or limitations of single-sequence mode co-folding for low-affinity ternary interfaces.
